### Individual / observational unit
- ==The person, object, or event described by one row of data==.
- ✓ Name the units that were actually observed or measured; a variable is a characteristic recorded for each unit.

### Variable
- ==A characteristic recorded for each individual or observational unit==.
- A variable takes different values across units.

### Categorical variable
- Records a label that places each unit into a group.
- Examples: phone type, color, or whether a person owns a smartphone.

### Quantitative variable
- Records ==a numerical count or measurement== for each unit.
- ✓ Classify a variable by what its values represent, not by whether the recorded values look numeric.

### Discrete variable
- A quantitative variable that takes separate, countable values, often whole-number counts.

### Continuous variable
- A quantitative variable that can take any value within an interval, such as weight, time, or length.

### Population
- ==The entire group of individuals or objects the study intends to describe==.

### Sample
- The ==individuals or objects from which data are actually collected==.

### Parameter
- A numerical summary of a characteristic of a population.

### Statistic
- A numerical summary calculated from a sample.
- ✓ A statistic describes a sample; a parameter describes a population.

### Census
- An attempt to collect data from ==every member of a population==.

### Statistical question
- A question that ==anticipates variability in data== and asks about a population or a relationship among variables.
- A question with one fixed answer about one individual is not statistical.

### Explanatory variable
- A variable used to explain or predict changes in a response variable.

### Response variable
- The outcome measured for each observational unit.

### Observational study
- Researchers observe units and measure variables ==without assigning a treatment==.
- ✓ An observed association alone ==does not establish that one variable causes another==.

### Experiment
- Researchers ==impose treatments== on experimental units and measure the responses.

### Random sample
- A sample ==selected using a chance process==.
- ✓ Random selection can support generalization to the population represented by the sampling process.

### Random assignment
- A chance process used to ==assign experimental units to treatments==.
- ✓ Random assignment can support cause-and-effect conclusions; it does not by itself support generalization.

### Confounding variable
- A variable ==associated with both group membership or the explanatory variable and the response==, making their separate effects difficult to distinguish.
- ! Explain how the variable relates to both the explanatory variable and the response.

### Distribution
- The values of a variable and how often or how densely those values occur.
