### Mean
- The arithmetic average: the ==sum of all observations divided by the number of observations==.
- The mean uses every value and is ==sensitive to skewness and outliers==.

### Median
- The ==middle value after observations are ordered==; if there are an even number of values, average the two middle values.
- ✓ The median is ==resistant to skewness and extreme values==.

### First quartile (Q1)
- The median of the lower half of the ordered data; about 25% of observations are at or below Q1.

### Third quartile (Q3)
- The median of the upper half of the ordered data; about 75% of observations are at or below Q3.

### Five-number summary
- The ==minimum, Q1, median, Q3, and maximum== of a quantitative data set.

### Interquartile range (IQR)
- The spread of the middle half of the data: **==IQR = Q3 − Q1==**.
- ✓ The IQR is ==resistant to skewness and extreme values==.

### Standard deviation (s or σ)
- A measure of the typical distance of observations from their mean, in the original units.
- ✓ Standard deviation describes spread around the mean and is sensitive to outliers.

### Range
- The maximum minus the minimum.
- The range depends only on the two extreme observations.

### Resistant summary
- A summary that is not greatly affected by extreme observations.
- Median and IQR are resistant; mean and standard deviation are not.

### 1.5 × IQR fences
- Lower fence: **==Q1 − 1.5 × IQR==**. Upper fence: **==Q3 + 1.5 × IQR==**.
- Values beyond a fence are flagged as possible outliers; the rule does not prove that a value is erroneous.

### z-score
- The ==number of standard deviations an observation lies above or below the mean==.
- A positive z-score is above the mean; a negative z-score is below the mean.

### Percentile
- The percent of observations at or below a specified value.

### Linear transformation
- A transformation of the form **x_new = a + b × x_old**.
- For b > 0, it shifts and rescales values without changing the distribution's shape or standardized scores.
