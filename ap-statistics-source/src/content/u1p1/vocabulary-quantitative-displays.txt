### Dotplot
- A display that places one dot at each observed quantitative value, stacking dots for repeated values.
- Useful for a small or moderate data set when individual observations matter.

### Stemplot
- A display that separates each value into a stem and a leaf while ==retaining the original observations==.
- ✓ Include a key with units and order leaves within each stem.

### Split stemplot
- A stemplot that repeats stems to spread out crowded leaves and reveal the distribution's shape.

### Back-to-back stemplot
- A display for comparing two quantitative distributions with a shared set of stems and ==leaves extending in opposite directions==.
- ✓ Use one key for each side and order leaves away from the stems.

### Histogram
- A display that ==groups quantitative values into intervals== and uses ==adjacent bars== to show frequencies or relative frequencies.
- ✓ Bars touch because the intervals represent a numerical scale.

### Bin / class interval
- A numerical interval used to group values in a histogram.
- Bin width and starting point affect the visible pattern.

### Boxplot
- A display ==based on the five-number summary==, with separate symbols for flagged outliers when applicable.
- A box extends from Q1 to Q3, with a line at the median; whiskers extend to the most extreme non-outlier values.

### Parallel boxplots
- Boxplots drawn on the same numerical scale to compare quantitative distributions.
- ✓ Use a common scale so relative centers and spreads are meaningful.

### Time plot
- A graph of a quantitative variable in time order.
- Preserving time order can reveal trends, cycles, or other changes over time.

### Shape
- The overall pattern of a quantitative distribution, including symmetry or skewness, peaks, and gaps.

### Symmetric distribution
- A distribution whose left and right sides are approximately mirror images around its center.

### Skewed right
- A distribution with a ==longer tail toward larger values==.
- ✓ The mean is often greater than the median because large values pull the mean right.

### Skewed left
- A distribution with a ==longer tail toward smaller values==.
- ✓ The mean is often less than the median because small values pull the mean left.

### Unimodal / bimodal
- Unimodal: one clear peak. Bimodal: two clear peaks.

### Gap
- An interval with no observations, or a noticeable break, within the range of a distribution.

### Outlier
- A value that lies unusually far from the rest of a distribution.
- Use the ==1.5 × IQR fences== as a consistent rule for flagging possible outliers.
