### What does an ==investigative question== guide? {days=1}

- “An investigative question guides ==the data to collect==, ==the methods used to analyze the data==, and ==the conclusions the study can support==.”
- **Key idea:** A statistical investigation uses data to answer a question about variation.

### What are the ==variables==, their types, and the ==experimental units==? {days=1}

- “The experimental units are **[individuals or objects assigned to treatments]**.”
- “The ==explanatory variable or factor== is **[factor]**, with levels **[list of levels or treatments]**.”
- “The ==response variable== is **[measured outcome]**.”
- “**[Variable]** is **[categorical / quantitative]**; if quantitative, it is **[discrete / continuous]** because it **[counts / measures ___]**.”
- **Key idea:** Experimental units receive treatments; the ==response variable== records the outcome. State the variable type from what it records.

### What is the ==study type==? {days=1,6}

| Study clue | Study type |
| --- | --- |
| Researchers ask people a ==common set of questions==. | Survey |
| Researchers measure variables and ==do not impose a treatment==. | Observational study |
| Researchers ==impose a treatment== on experimental units. | Experiment |
- **Key idea:** A ==survey is an observational study==.
- **Key idea:** ==Random assignment is a separate design feature== that supports cause-and-effect conclusions.

### Why is this an ==observational study==? {days=1}

- “This is an observational study because researchers measured **[variables]** but ==did not impose a treatment or assign units to treatments==; the ==variables occurred naturally==.”
- **Key idea:** An observed association alone ==does not establish causation==.

### What is a possible ==confounding variable==? {days=1}

- “**[Confounder]** is a possible ==confounding variable== because it is ==associated with== **[explanatory variable]**: **[describe the relationship]**.”
- “It is also ==associated with== **[==response variable==]**: **[describe the relationship]**.”
- “If both relationships hold, we could observe an ==association between== **[explanatory variable]** and **[==response variable==]** even if **[explanatory variable]** has no effect on **[==response variable==]**.”
- “Therefore, this study ==does not establish causation== between **[explanatory variable]** and **[==response variable==]**.”
- **Key idea:** A confounder must be ==associated with both the explanatory and response variables==; describe each relationship.

### What are the ==sample== and ==population==? {days=1,6}

- “The sample is **[the individuals or objects from which data were collected]**.”
- “The population is **[==the entire group the study intends to describe==]**.”
- **Key idea:** Distinguish the ==individuals or objects actually sampled== from ==the group the study intends to describe==.

### To which ==population== can these results be generalized? {days=1}

- “Because the sample was ==randomly selected from== **[population]**, the results can be ==generalized to== **[that population]**.”
- “Because the participants were not ==randomly selected from== a population, the results ==apply most directly to people similar to== **[the participants]**.”
- **Key idea:** ==Random selection supports generalization==.
- **Key idea:** ==Random assignment supports cause-and-effect conclusions==.

### What is the ==investigative question== for this study? {days=1}

- “For **[population]**, does **[treatment/==explanatory variable==]** **[change/increase/decrease]** **[==response parameter==]** compared with **[==comparison level==]**?”
- **Key idea:** Include the population, variable(s), and ==target parameter or comparison==; make sure data can answer the question.

### What question can ==these data== answer? {days=1}

- “Among **[population represented by the study]**, is there an ==association between== **[variable 1]** and **[variable 2]**?”
- **Key idea:** ==Use only variables actually recorded==. Describe an association ==without claiming causation==.

### What are the ==three== components of an investigative question? {days=2,5,6}

**1. Variables of interest:** “The variable(s) of interest are **[variable(s)]**. For a comparison, the ==response variable== is **[outcome]** and the ==explanatory variable or factor== is **[factor]**.”

**2. Type of inference / parameter:** Choose the stem that matches the study goal.

| Inference goal | Answer stem |
| --- | --- |
| Estimate | We want to estimate the ==[population proportion/mean]== of **[variable]** among **[population]**. |
| Test a claim | We want to test the claim that the ==[population proportion/mean]== of **[variable]** equals **[claimed value]**. |
| Compare groups | We want to compare the ==[population proportions/means]== of the ==response variable== between **[groups]**. |
| Assess association | We want to assess the ==association between== **[variable 1]** and **[variable 2]** among **[population]**. |

**3. Type of conclusion:** Select the conclusion supported by the sampling and assignment methods.

| Conclusion | Answer stem |
| --- | --- |
| Generalization | “The sample was **[randomly / not randomly]** ==selected from== **[population]**. Therefore, the results **[can / cannot]** be ==generalized to== **[that population]**.” |
| Cause and effect | “The study **[did / did not]** ==randomly assign units to treatments==. Because the result **[was / was not]** statistically significant, **[a cause-and-effect conclusion is / is not]** justified.” |
| Both | “The sample was ==randomly selected from== **[population]**, and the study ==randomly assigned units to treatments==. The results can be generalized to **[population]**, and the statistically significant treatment effect supports a cause-and-effect conclusion.” |

**Key idea:** The three parts state the variables, the statistical goal, and the conclusion the study design can support.

### What are the ==observational units== and variable types? {days=1}

- “The observational units are **[==individuals or objects described by the data==]**.”
- “The variables are **[list]**.”
- “**[Variable]** is categorical because it records **[==a label or category==]**.”
- “**[Variable]** is quantitative and **[==discrete/continuous==]** because it **[==counts/measures== ___]**.”
- **Key idea:** ==Observational units are the individuals or objects described==; ==variables are the characteristics recorded== for them.
