### Decomposition reaction
- One reactant forms two or more products.
- Example pattern: \(AB \rightarrow A + B\).

### Precipitation reaction
- Dissolved ions combine to form an ==insoluble ionic solid==.
- A solid by itself does not prove a precipitation reaction; a metal can also form through redox.

### Single-replacement reaction
- One element reacts with a compound and replaces another element in that compound.
- Many metal and halogen replacement reactions involve electron transfer and are redox reactions.

### Oxidation and reduction
- Oxidation is loss of electrons; reduction is gain of electrons.
- ✓ Track which species loses electrons and which gains them; both changes occur in a redox reaction.

### Acid–carbonate reaction
- An acid supplies \(H^+\), which reacts with carbonate to produce \(CO_2\) and \(H_2O\).
- Bubbles are evidence of gas formation; identify the gas and explain how it forms.

### Catalyst
- A substance that provides a reaction pathway with lower activation energy and is regenerated overall.
- A catalyst changes the rate, not the net reaction or the identity of the products.

### Expected evidence
- A predicted observation is evidence the model says should occur; it is not a report of an observation that was never measured.
- ✓ Label evidence as observed or expected when the prompt is asking for a prediction.
