### Draw an ionic particle diagram: sodium chloride (NaCl)
- Step: Draw a separate bracketed sodium ion with a positive charge and a bracketed chloride ion with a negative charge.
- Step: Show eight valence-electron dots around chloride; sodium has lost its outer electron.
- Example: \([\mathrm{Na}]^+[\mathrm{Cl}]^-\); the ions combine in a one-to-one ratio to make neutral \(\mathrm{NaCl}\).
- Check: Show the particles as ions, not as a covalent molecule.
- Visual: ionic-drawing

### Draw isotope particle diagrams: hydrogen
- Step: Draw one proton in each hydrogen nucleus.
- Step: Add zero, one, or two neutrons for hydrogen-1, hydrogen-2, or hydrogen-3.
- Step: Add one electron for each neutral hydrogen atom.
- Check: Isotopes of one element have the same proton count and different neutron counts.
- Visual: isotopes

### Write a full electron configuration: magnesium
- Step: Use magnesium's atomic number to determine that a neutral atom has 12 electrons.
- Step: Fill orbitals in increasing energy order; do not skip an available lower-energy orbital.
- Example: \(1s^2\,2s^2\,2p^6\,3s^2\).
- Check: The superscripts add to 12 electrons.
- Visual: orbitals

### Draw an orbital diagram: oxygen
- Step: Write the full configuration \(1s^2\,2s^2\,2p^4\).
- Step: Draw one box for each orbital; draw electrons as arrows.
- Step: In the three equal-energy \(2p\) orbitals, place one upward arrow in each box before pairing the fourth electron.
- Check: A paired orbital has opposite-spin arrows; the diagram contains eight electrons.
- Visual: oxygen-orbital

### Draw a Lewis structure: water (H₂O)
- Step: Count eight valence electrons: six from oxygen and one from each hydrogen.
- Step: Put oxygen in the center and connect each hydrogen to it with a single bond.
- Step: Place the four remaining electrons as two lone pairs on oxygen.
- Check: Count eight electrons total; each hydrogen has two shared electrons and oxygen has an octet.
- Visual: lewis-water

### Draw the resonance structures of ozone (O₃)
- Step: Count 18 valence electrons and connect the three oxygen atoms in a row.
- Step: Draw one double bond and one single bond; place lone pairs to complete the terminal oxygen octets.
- Step: Show the second form by moving electrons, not atoms; include the formal charges.
- Check: The two forms keep the same oxygen positions and have an overall charge of zero.
- Visual: resonance

### Read a photoelectron spectrum: sodium
- Step: Label the horizontal axis binding energy and the vertical axis relative electron count.
- Step: Match the peaks to the occupied subshells \(1s^2\), \(2s^2\), \(2p^6\), and \(3s^1\).
- Step: Use peak position for binding energy and peak size for the number of electrons in that subshell.
- Check: The peak counts add to 11 electrons; the outermost electron has the lowest binding energy.
- Visual: photoelectron-spectrum

### Model three-dimensional molecular geometry
- Step: Draw a Lewis structure and count electron domains around the central atom.
- Step: Use a three-dimensional arrangement that keeps the domains as far apart as possible.
- Example: Methane \((\mathrm{CH_4})\) is tetrahedral; ammonia \((\mathrm{NH_3})\) is trigonal pyramidal; water \((\mathrm{H_2O})\) is bent; boron trifluoride \((\mathrm{BF_3})\) is trigonal planar.
- Check: Rotate the model to inspect the shape from different angles; lone pairs affect the shape but are not named as atoms.
- Visual: tetrahedral-3d
