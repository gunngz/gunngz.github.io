### Coulombic attraction
- Definition: Electrostatic attraction between ==opposite charges==, such as the nucleus and an electron.
- Relation: \(\mathrm{attraction}\propto\frac{|\mathrm{charge}_1\mathrm{charge}_2|}{\mathrm{distance}^2}\); larger charge or shorter distance means stronger attraction.
- Visual: coulomb

### Effective nuclear charge
- Definition: The ==net positive charge== experienced by an electron after shielding by other electrons.
- Relation: \(\mathrm{effective\ nuclear\ charge}\approx\mathrm{nuclear\ charge}-\mathrm{shielding}\).
- Example: Across a period, nuclear charge increases while shielding changes little, so outer electrons feel stronger attraction.
- Visual: coulomb

### Shielding
- Definition: Core electrons ==reduce an outer electron's attraction to the nucleus==.
- Relation: Down a group, additional inner shells increase shielding and electron distance.
- Visual: coulomb

### Atomic radius
- Definition: A measure of ==atomic size==.
- Relation: Radius generally decreases across a period and increases down a group; it grows toward the lower left of the periodic table.
- Visual: periodic-trend

### First ionization energy
- Definition: Energy required to ==remove one electron== from a gaseous atom.
- Example: \(\mathrm{Li(g)}\rightarrow\mathrm{Li^+(g)}+e^-\).
- Relation: It generally increases toward the upper right of the periodic table as attraction strengthens.
- Visual: periodic-trend

### Electronegativity
- Definition: An atom's ==attraction for shared electrons== in a bond.
- Relation: It generally increases toward the upper right of the periodic table.
- Visual: periodic-trend

### Periodic-trend explanation
- Definition: Explain a trend by connecting electron distance and shielding to ==Coulombic attraction==.
- Key idea: State the direction, then explain the particle-level cause; an arrow alone is not an explanation.
- Visual: coulomb

### Ion charge and valence electrons
- Definition: Main-group atoms ==gain or lose electrons== toward a stable outer-shell arrangement.
- Relation: \(\mathrm{ion\ charge}=\#\mathrm{protons}-\#e^-\); the charges in a neutral ionic formula add to zero.
- Example: Sodium loses one electron to form \(\mathrm{Na^+}\); chlorine gains one to form \(\mathrm{Cl^-}\).
- Visual: ion
