### London dispersion force
- Definition: Attraction from ==temporary and induced dipoles==.
- Example: Iodine molecules have more polarizable electron clouds and stronger dispersion attractions than fluorine molecules.
- Key idea: Present between all atoms and molecules; stronger attractions generally raise boiling point and viscosity and lower vapor pressure.
- Visual: dispersion

### Dipole–dipole attraction
- Definition: Attraction between the ==partial positive and partial negative== ends of neighboring polar molecules.
- Example: The partially positive hydrogen of one hydrogen chloride molecule attracts the partially negative chlorine of another.
- Visual: dipole-pair

### Hydrogen bonding
- Definition: A strong dipole–dipole attraction when ==hydrogen bonded to nitrogen, oxygen, or fluorine== is attracted to a lone pair on nitrogen, oxygen, or fluorine.
- Example: In liquid water, a hydrogen bonded to oxygen is attracted to a lone pair on a neighboring oxygen.
- Key idea: Hydrogen alone is not enough; check both the atom bonded to hydrogen and the atom with the lone pair.
- Visual: hydrogen-bond

### Ion–dipole attraction
- Definition: Attraction between ==an ion and the partial charges== of a polar molecule.
- Example: Water's partially negative oxygen points toward \(\mathrm{Na^+}\); its partially positive hydrogens point toward \(\mathrm{Cl^-}\).
- Key idea: This attraction helps water dissolve sodium chloride.
- Visual: ion-dipole
