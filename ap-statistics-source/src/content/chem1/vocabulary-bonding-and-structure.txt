### Ionic bonding
- Definition: Electrostatic attraction between ==oppositely charged ions== in an extended lattice.
- Example: Solid sodium chloride contains alternating \(\mathrm{Na^+}\) and \(\mathrm{Cl^-}\) ions; it is not made of separate molecules.
- Relation: The formula \(\mathrm{NaCl}\) shows the simplest neutral ion ratio.
- Visual: ionic-lattice

### Covalent bond
- Definition: A bond formed when atoms ==share one or more pairs of electrons==.
- Example: In \(\mathrm{H_2}\), two hydrogen atoms share one pair of electrons.
- Key idea: Covalent substances can be separate molecules, such as water, or extended networks, such as diamond.
- Visual: covalent-bond

### Formal charge
- Definition: A ==bookkeeping charge== assigned to an atom in a Lewis structure.
- Relation: \(\mathrm{formal\ charge}=\mathrm{valence\ electrons}-\mathrm{nonbonding\ electrons}-\frac{1}{2}(\mathrm{bonding\ electrons})\).
- Example: In water, oxygen has two nonbonding pairs and two bonds; use the electron count to check its formal charge.
- Visual: lewis-water

### Resonance structures
- Definition: Lewis structures with the ==same atom arrangement== but different electron placement.
- Example: The two resonance structures of ozone keep the three oxygen atoms in the same positions while the double bond changes sides.
- Key idea: Atoms do not move between resonance forms; only electrons move.
- Visual: resonance

### Electron-domain geometry
- Definition: The arrangement of ==all electron domains== around a central atom, including bonds and lone pairs.
- Relation: Count each bond or lone pair as one electron domain to determine the geometry.
- Example: Carbon in methane has four bonding domains arranged tetrahedrally.
- Visual: geometry

### Molecular geometry
- Definition: The ==three-dimensional arrangement of bonded atoms==; lone pairs affect shape but are not named as atoms.
- Example: Methane \((\mathrm{CH_4})\) is tetrahedral with bond angles near \(109.5^\circ\); water \((\mathrm{H_2O})\) is bent.
- Key idea: Use molecular geometry for questions about shape, bond angles, or molecular polarity.
- Visual: tetrahedral-3d

### Electron-domain repulsion model
- Definition: Predicts molecular shape by treating electron domains as ==regions that repel one another==.
- Example: Three bonding domains and one lone pair form a trigonal-pyramidal molecule, such as ammonia \((\mathrm{NH_3})\).
- Key idea: Lone pairs occupy more space than bonding domains and can compress bond angles.
- Visual: tetrahedral-3d

### Hybridization
- Definition: A model describing ==mixed atomic orbitals== at a central atom.
- Relation: If requested, match the label used in class to the number of electron domains.
- Visual: orbitals
