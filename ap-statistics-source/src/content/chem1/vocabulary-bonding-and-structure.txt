### Ionic bonding
- Electrostatic attraction between oppositely charged ions in an extended lattice.
- An ionic solid is not made of separate molecules; its formula gives the simplest neutral ratio of ions.

### Covalent bond
- A bond formed when atoms share one or more pairs of electrons.
- A covalent substance may consist of discrete molecules or an extended network.

### Lewis structure
- A representation of valence electrons as bonds and lone pairs.
- It shows connectivity and electron placement, but a two-dimensional Lewis structure alone does not show the full three-dimensional molecular shape.

### Chemical nomenclature
- A system for naming substances from their composition and bonding type.
- For an ionic compound, the formula must have ==zero total charge==; for a molecular compound, prefixes specify atom counts.

### Formal charge
- A bookkeeping charge assigned to an atom in a Lewis structure.
- \(FC = V - N - \frac{B}{2}\), where \(V\) is the atom's valence-electron count, \(N\) is its nonbonding-electron count, and \(B\) is the number of bonding electrons.

### Resonance structures
- Lewis structures with the same atom arrangement but different placement of electrons.
- The atoms do not move between resonance forms; only electron placement changes.

### Electron-domain geometry
- The arrangement of all electron domains around a central atom, including bonds and lone pairs.
- Use the number of domains to determine the electron-domain geometry.

### Molecular geometry
- The three-dimensional arrangement of the bonded atoms; lone pairs affect the shape but are not named as atoms in the molecular geometry.
- ✓ Use the molecular structure when the question asks about shape, bond angles, or molecular polarity.

### VSEPR model
- A model that predicts geometry by treating electron domains around a central atom as regions that repel one another.
- Lone pairs occupy more space than bonding domains and can compress bond angles.

### Hybridization
- A bonding model that labels mixed atomic orbitals at a central atom.
- If a class question asks for it, match the hybridization label to the number of electron domains; do not add hybridization to an explanation unless it is requested or useful.
