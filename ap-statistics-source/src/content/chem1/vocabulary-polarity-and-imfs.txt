### Polar bond
- A covalent bond with an unequal distribution of shared electrons because the bonded atoms have different electronegativities.
- The bond dipole points toward the more electronegative atom.

### Molecular polarity
- The overall distribution of charge in a molecule, determined by its bond dipoles and three-dimensional structure.
- A molecule can contain polar bonds and still be nonpolar if the dipoles cancel by symmetry.

### Dipole moment
- A measure of the separation of positive and negative charge.
- Molecular dipoles combine as vectors; their directions matter, not just their magnitudes.

### London dispersion force
- An intermolecular attraction caused by temporary and induced dipoles.
- It occurs between all atoms and molecules and generally strengthens as electron clouds become more polarizable.

### Dipole–dipole attraction
- An attraction between the partially positive and partially negative ends of neighboring polar molecules.

### Hydrogen bonding
- A particularly strong dipole–dipole attraction when hydrogen bonded to N, O, or F interacts with a lone pair on N, O, or F.
- ✓ Identify the required atoms and the intermolecular attraction; the presence of hydrogen alone is not enough.

### Ion–dipole attraction
- An attraction between an ion and the partial charges of a polar molecule.
- It is important when explaining how many ionic substances interact with polar solvents such as water.

### Intermolecular forces (IMFs)
- Attractions between separate particles; they are different from the bonds within a molecule or ionic lattice.
- Stronger IMFs generally correspond to higher boiling point, higher viscosity, and lower vapor pressure for comparable substances.

### Solubility and polarity
- Dissolution is favored when solute–solvent attractions can compensate for attractions within the original solute and solvent.
- Use the specific structures and attractions in the question; “like dissolves like” is a starting clue, not a complete explanation.
