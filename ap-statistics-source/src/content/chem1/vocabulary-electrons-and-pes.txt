### Ground-state electron configuration
- The arrangement of electrons with the ==lowest total energy== for an atom or ion.
- A configuration must contain the correct total number of electrons.

### Aufbau principle
- Electrons occupy available orbitals in order of ==increasing energy==.

### Pauli exclusion principle
- An orbital holds at most two electrons, and two electrons in the same orbital have ==opposite spins==.

### Hund's rule
- In a set of equal-energy orbitals, electrons occupy separate orbitals before they pair.
- Unpaired electrons in these orbitals have parallel spins.

### Valence electrons
- Electrons in the outermost occupied shell of a main-group atom.
- Valence electrons are central to typical ion charges and chemical bonding.

### Ion electron configuration
- A cation has lost electrons; an anion has gained electrons.
- When forming a transition-metal cation, remove electrons from the ==highest principal energy level first==, even if those electrons were added last.

### Photoelectron spectroscopy (PES)
- A method that measures the energy needed to remove electrons from atoms or ions.
- A PES peak identifies electrons with similar binding energies; peak area or relative height represents the number of electrons in that group.

### Binding energy
- The energy required to remove an electron from an atom or ion.
- Electrons with greater binding energy are ==held more strongly==; read the axis direction before comparing peak positions.

### Ground state and excited state
- A ground-state arrangement has the lowest possible energy for that electron count.
- An excited-state arrangement has the same electron count but places at least one electron at a higher energy.
