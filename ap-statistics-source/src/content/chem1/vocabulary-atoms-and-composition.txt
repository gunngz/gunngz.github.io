### Atomic number
- The number of ==protons in the nucleus==; it identifies the element.
- In a neutral atom, the number of electrons equals the atomic number.

### Mass number
- The total number of ==protons and neutrons== in one isotope.
- Neutrons = mass number − atomic number.

### Isotope
- Atoms of the same element with the same number of protons but ==different numbers of neutrons==.
- Isotopes can have different masses while retaining the same chemical identity.

### Average atomic mass
- The ==weighted average== of the masses of an element's naturally occurring isotopes.
- Use each isotope's fractional abundance as its weight; do not take an unweighted average unless abundances are equal.

### Mass spectrum
- A graph of ion signal versus mass-to-charge ratio, \(m/z\).
- For singly charged isotope ions, peak position indicates isotope mass and relative peak intensity indicates relative abundance.

### Mole
- \(6.022\times10^{23}\) specified particles.
- State what the particles are: atoms, molecules, ions, or formula units.

### Molar mass
- The mass of one mole of a substance, in \(\mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}}\).
- Calculate it by adding the atomic masses for every atom in the chemical formula.

### Percent composition
- The mass of one element divided by the total sample mass, multiplied by 100%.
- A compound's percent composition is fixed; a mixture's composition can vary.

### Empirical formula
- The ==lowest whole-number ratio== of atoms or ions in a substance.
- Ionic formulas are written as the simplest ratio that makes the total charge zero.

### Molecular formula
- The actual number of each type of atom in one molecule.
- It is a whole-number multiple of the empirical formula when the substance is molecular.
