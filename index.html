Creek

Response
Culturally, the Creek were southeastern descendants of Mississippian peoples whose dense communities were supported by maize, beans, and squash. Politically, they lived within the Southeast’s network of chiefdoms and later became one of the major regional peoples encountered by Europeans.
Evidence1 quote

5. Spain’s Rise to Global Leadership1 prompts
Response
The marriage of Ferdinand of Aragon and Isabella of Castile united Spain’s major kingdoms. Their completion of the Reconquista in 1492 expelled the Muslim kingdom of Granada and released military and religious energy for overseas conquest. Renaissance curiosity, new navigational knowledge, and rivalry with Portugal encouraged exploration. Columbus’s voyage gave Spain enormous western claims, and the Treaty of Tordesillas divided non-Christian lands between Spain and Portugal, awarding Spain most of the Americas.

6. Conquistadores, the New World, and the Global Economy1 prompts
Response
European contact carried smallpox and other diseases to peoples without immunity, causing catastrophic population collapse. Spanish soldiers conquered Native states and missionaries spread Christianity. Europeans introduced horses, cattle, pigs, wheat, and sugar; the Americas supplied Europe with maize, potatoes, tobacco, beans, squash, and cacao. Plantation agriculture and mining demanded labor, encouraging encomienda, Native enslavement, and the transatlantic slave trade. American silver entered European markets and financed trade with Asia, while the Columbian Exchange permanently joined the Atlantic world.
Evidence3 quotes
“Columbus’s discovery initiated the kind of explosion in international commerce that a later age would call ‘globalization.’”
“The Indians’ stunningly swift conquest was due in large measure to the smallpox epidemic that decimated Native communities.”
“Yet the invaders brought more than conquest and death. They brought crops and animals, language and laws, customs and religion, all of which proved adaptable to the peoples of the Americas.”

Response
Conquest transferred enormous stores of Indigenous gold and silver—especially from the Aztec and Inca empires—to Spain. American bullion expanded Europe’s money supply, stimulated commerce and investment, and paid for Asian goods, helping create a world economy and strengthening early capitalism.

9. “New Spain” and the Origins of Modern Mexican Culture1 prompts
Response
Spanish colonies were “New Spain” because Spaniards imposed their monarchy, Catholic religion, language, law, labor systems, and commercial priorities. They were not merely copies of Europe, however. Disease, conquest, Indigenous survival, and intermarriage produced mestizo communities. Mexico thus became a hybrid of Spanish and Indigenous peoples, foods, languages, religious practices, laws, and memories rather than a purely European society.
Evidence2 quotes
“Especially in Mexico, they intermarried with the surviving Indigenous peoples, creating a distinctive culture of mestizos, people of mixed Indigenous and European heritage.”
“To this day Mexico remains a unique blend of the Old World and the New, producing both ambivalence and pride among its people.”
MapMexico, Spain

2. Limits on French Growth1 prompts
Response
New France grew slowly because France had a relatively small population, French peasants generally owned land and had less reason to migrate, and the crown discouraged Huguenot settlement. Many French resources went to more profitable Caribbean sugar islands. New France emphasized male fur traders and scattered Jesuit missions rather than large family migration, agriculture, and self-government.

Jamestown stood on a disease-ridden, mosquito-infested site with brackish water. Response

6. The Great Awakening1 prompts
Response
The Great Awakening of the 1730s and 1740s rejected cold formalism and emphasized emotional conversion, human dependence on divine grace, itinerant preaching, and individual spiritual experience. Jonathan Edwards used rigorous Calvinist theology and terrifying images of damnation; George Whitefield used dramatic open-air oratory to reach enormous interracial and intercolonial audiences. New Lights defended revival, while Old Lights criticized emotional excess. The movement increased church membership and denominational diversity, founded new schools, weakened established clergy, encouraged ordinary people to question authority, and created a shared experience across colonial boundaries.

Regional Comparison

Response
New England had rocky soil, cold weather, compact towns, family farms, fishing, shipbuilding, commerce, Congregationalism, high literacy, town meetings, long life expectancy, and relatively stable families.
