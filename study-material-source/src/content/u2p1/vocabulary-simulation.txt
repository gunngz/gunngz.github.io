### Probability {days=2,3}
- A number from 0 to 1 describing the long-run proportion of times an outcome occurs in a chance process.
- A probability of 0.25 means the event occurs about 1 out of every 4 trials in the long run; it does not guarantee exactly 25 out of 100 in a small sample.

### Simulation {days=2}
- A way to imitate a chance process using a model that assigns outcomes according to the probabilities in the real situation.
- One ==repetition== is a complete simulated run with one recorded result. It can contain several individual draws or attempts.
- Homework often calls one complete repetition a ==trial==; the PPT also uses “trials” for individual attempts within a repetition.

- Sources: U2P1 Day 2, slide 4; U2 HW #2, §2B #9, 11–12.

### With or without replacement {days=2,3,4,5}
- ==With replacement==: return an item before the next draw. In a well-mixed random draw, the probabilities stay the same.
- ==Without replacement==: keep the item out. The remaining counts change, so later probabilities can change.
- In a simulation with ==one label per individual==, reject repeated labels within a sample without replacement. Reset the full set for the next repetition.
- When codes stand for ==outcome types== rather than individuals, repeated codes can be valid. Several different people can have the same outcome.

- Homework source: Simulation Worksheet #3–4; U2 HW #2, §2B #11–12, 16; Probability Level-Up, Yellow Belt #2, 4.

### Stopping rule {days=2}
- The condition that tells you ==when one complete simulation repetition ends==.
- Examples include reaching the first success, reaching a target total, or reaching a maximum number of attempts.
- State ==what is recorded== at the end: a count, a total, or whether an event occurred.

- Homework source: Simulation Worksheet #2, 5.

### Convincing evidence {days=2}
- A result that is ==unlikely to occur by chance alone if the claim is true== provides evidence against that claim.
- Use results ==as extreme as or more extreme than the observed result== in the direction being investigated.
- A result that is common under the claim ==does not prove the claim true==.

- Homework source: U2 HW #2, §2B #9, 11–12, 16.

### Empirical probability {days=2}
- An estimate based on the observed proportion of outcomes from repeated trials.
- For a fair coin, the proportion of heads varies in the short run and tends toward 0.5 as the number of tosses grows.
- It is ==based on observed results==, unlike a theoretical probability based on a model.

- Homework source: U2 HW #2, §2B #5(b).

### Theoretical probability {days=2}
- A probability determined from a chance model, before using results from repeated trials.
- For a fair coin, \(P(\text{heads})=0.5\).

### Law of Large Numbers {days=2}
- As the number of independent repetitions increases, the proportion of times an outcome occurs tends to get closer to its probability.
- It describes long-run behavior; it does not make a future trial compensate for earlier results.
