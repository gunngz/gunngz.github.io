### False positive {days=2,4,5}
- A test indicates that a condition is present when the condition is actually absent.
- For condition \(D\) and a positive result \(+\), the ==false-positive rate== is \(P(+\mid D^c)\).

- Homework source: U2 HW #2, §2B #4; U2 HW #5, §2D #26.

### False negative {days=2,4,5}
- A test indicates that a condition is absent when the condition is actually present.
- For condition \(D\), the ==false-negative rate== is \(P(-\mid D)\).
- \(P(D^c\mid +)\) is a different conditional probability: it asks whether the condition is absent given a positive result.

- Homework source: U2 HW #5, §2D #25–26.

### Sensitivity and specificity {days=5}
Let \(D\) mean “has the condition.”

- ==Sensitivity==: the chance of a positive test ==given the condition is present==.

\[\text{sensitivity}=P(+\mid D)\]

- ==Specificity==: the chance of a negative test ==given the condition is absent==.

\[\text{specificity}=P(-\mid D^c)\]

![Diagnostic test outcomes: true and false positives and negatives, with the actual condition defining the groups used for sensitivity and specificity.](./diagrams/u2p1-diagnostic-testing.svg)

- A test's sensitivity is not \(P(D\mid +)\), the chance of having the condition after a positive result.
- Homework source: U2 HW #5, §2D #25–26.
