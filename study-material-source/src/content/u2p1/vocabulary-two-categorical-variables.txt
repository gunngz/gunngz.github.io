### Explanatory and response variables {days=1}
- The ==explanatory variable== is used to explain or predict differences in the response variable.
- The ==response variable== is the outcome measured for each observational unit.
- In the Yellowstone example, environmental club membership is explanatory; snowmobile use is the response.

### Two-way table {days=1}
- A table that organizes counts for ==every combination of two categorical variables==.
- An interior cell gives the count for one pair of categories; row and column totals are marginal counts.

### Marginal relative frequency {days=1}
- A row or column total divided by the ==overall sample size==.
- It describes the distribution of ==one variable== in the full sample.
- The marginal relative frequencies for a variable sum to 1 (or 100%).

### Joint relative frequency {days=1}
- An interior cell count divided by the ==overall sample size==.
- It describes observations in ==both categories at the same time==.

### Conditional relative frequency {days=1}
- A cell count divided by the total for the ==given group==.
- It describes the distribution of one variable among observations meeting a condition on the other variable.
- Use conditional relative frequencies to compare groups and assess association.

### Segmented bar chart {days=1}
- A display of conditional distributions using ==equal-width bars==, one for each explanatory-variable category.
- Each bar represents 100%; its segments show the conditional proportions of response categories.

### Mosaic plot {days=1}
- A display of two categorical variables in which bar widths are proportional to explanatory-group sizes.
- Segment heights show conditional proportions; each rectangle's area represents a joint proportion.
- Unlike a segmented bar chart, a mosaic plot has ==different-width bars with no gaps==.

### Association between categorical variables {days=1}
- There is an association when the ==conditional distributions differ== across groups.
- An association can help predict the response from the explanatory variable, but it does not by itself establish causation.
