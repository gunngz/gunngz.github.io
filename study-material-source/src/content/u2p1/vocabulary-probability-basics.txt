### Probability model {days=3}
- A description of chance behavior that lists the possible outcomes and gives the probability of each outcome or event.
- Each probability is between 0 and 1, and the probabilities of all outcomes in the sample space sum to 1.
- ==Equally likely== means every individual outcome has the same probability. Use “favorable outcomes divided by all outcomes” only when the listed outcomes are equally likely.

- Homework source: U2 HW #3, §2C #1, 3, 5(a).

### Sample space {days=3}
- The set of ==all possible outcomes== of a random phenomenon, written \(S\).
- For one roll of a six-sided die, \(S=\{1,2,3,4,5,6\}\).

### Event {days=3}
- Any collection of outcomes from a random phenomenon; an event is a ==subset of the sample space==.
- If \(A\) is the event of rolling a 6, then \(A=\{6\}\).

### Venn diagram {days=3}
- A diagram that represents the sample space and events as regions; overlapping regions show outcomes shared by events.
- Use the overlap for outcomes in both events and the combined regions for outcomes in either event.

### Frequency table {days=3}
- A table that organizes counts for categories or combinations of categories.
- To find a probability from counts, divide the count in the requested region by the total number of observational units.

### Complement {days=3}
- The event that \(A\) does not occur, written \(A^c\), \(A'\), or \(\overline{A}\).
- \(P(A^c)=1-P(A)\).

### Mutually exclusive (disjoint) events {days=3,4,5}
- Events that ==cannot occur at the same time==; \(P(A\cap B)=0\).
- For mutually exclusive events, \(P(A\cup B)=P(A)+P(B)\).
- ==Disjoint== is another word for mutually exclusive. Events with nonzero probabilities that are mutually exclusive are ==not independent==: one occurring rules out the other.

- Homework source: Probability Level-Up, Yellow Belt #5; U2 HW #5, §2D #39.

### Intersection (joint probability) {days=3}
- The probability that two events occur ==simultaneously==, written \(P(A\cap B)\).
- The ==intersection== contains outcomes in ==both A and B==.

### Union {days=3}
- The event ==A or B or both==, written \(A\cup B\).
- “Or” includes the overlap unless the question says ==exactly one== or ==but not both==.

- Homework source: U2 HW #3–4, §2C #15–18, 23–24; Probability Level-Up, White Belt #8–9.

### Probability wording {days=2,3,4,5}
Let \(X\) be the count being recorded.

| Wording | Event |
| --- | --- |
| ==At least k==; k or more | \(X\geq k\) |
| ==At most k==; k or fewer | \(X\leq k\) |
| Exactly k | \(X=k\) |
| More than k | \(X>k\) |
| Fewer than k | \(X<k\) |
| Both A and B | \(A\cap B\) |
| A or B, or both | \(A\cup B\) |
| Only A | \(A\cap B^c\) |
| Neither A nor B | \(A^c\cap B^c=(A\cup B)^c\) |

- Homework source: U2 HW #2, §2B #9, 11–12; U2 HW #3, §2C #3, 9, 11, 23–24; Probability Level-Up, Green Belt.

### General addition rule {days=3}
- For any two events, \(P(A\cup B)=P(A)+P(B)-P(A\cap B)\).
- Subtract the overlap once so outcomes in both events are not counted twice.
