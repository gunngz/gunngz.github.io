### Binomial random variable {days=2}
- \(X\) counts successes in a fixed number \(n\) of trials that meet **BITS**.
- Write \(X\sim\operatorname{Bin}(n,p)\), where \(p\) is the success probability on ==one trial==.
- Possible values are \(0,1,\ldots,n\).
- **PPT source** · Day 2, slides 4–7.

### BITS conditions {days=2}
| Letter | Condition |
| --- | --- |
| B · Binary | Each trial is classified as a success or a failure. |
| I · Independent | The outcome of a trial does not affect the probabilities on other trials. |
| T · Trials | The number of trials is fixed in advance. |
| S · Same probability | Each trial has the same probability of success. |

- **PPT source** · Day 2, slides 4–5.

### Binomial coefficient {days=2}
- \(\binom{n}{x}\), read “\(n\) choose \(x\),” counts the ways to place \(x\) successes among \(n\) trials.
- This factor combines all success-and-failure orders that give the requested count.
- **PPT source** · Day 2, slides 6–7.

### 10% condition {days=2}
- When sampling ==without replacement==, check whether \(n\le 0.10N\), where \(N\) is the population size.
- If it holds, treating the draws as approximately independent is reasonable for these calculations. The corresponding binomial model is an approximation.
- **PPT source** · Day 2, slide 16.

### Shape of a binomial distribution {days=2}
- For the same \(n\), small \(p\) tends to give right skew, \(p=0.5\) gives symmetry, and large \(p\) tends to give left skew.
- **PPT source** · Day 2, slides 13–15.
