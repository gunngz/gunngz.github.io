### Trials in BITS
- **Trials** means the ==number of trials==, \(n\), fixed in advance.
- **Population size**, \(N\), counts the individuals in the population.
- State how many times the trial is repeated. For example, testing 30 oranges involves 30 trials, even if the factory produces thousands of oranges.

### Label every tree branch and complete path
- Write the ==corresponding probability on every branch==.
- For a later branch, use the probability conditional on the earlier outcome.
- At the end of each complete path, write the ==product of all branch probabilities along that path==.

\[P(A\cap B)=P(A)P(B\mid A)\]

### Write the probability statement
- Explicitly write ==\(P(\ldots)\)== to identify the event before showing the calculation and result.
- Match the symbols to the wording, including the boundary in “at least” or “at most.”

\[P(X\ge2)=P(X=2)+P(X=3)+\cdots\]

### Use the full context
- Describe the ==complete population and the characteristic being studied==.
- For an orange-mass question, use wording such as “the mean mass of ==all oranges produced by the factory==.” Include any production period or other scope given in the question.
- Connect every explanation and conclusion to the specific population, variable, and units in the problem.

### Draw and label every Normal curve
- Draw a Normal curve for ==every related question== and shade the requested region.
- Label the variable and its ==units==, the ==mean==, and the values ==one, two, and three standard deviations on each side of the mean==.
- Mark the relevant value or values and their corresponding ==z-scores==.

\[z=\frac{x-\mu}{\sigma}\]

- In the illustrative orange-mass model below, the mean is 100 g and the SD is 10 g. A mass of 120 g has \(z=2\). The shaded region represents \(P(X>120)\).

### Define bias using the sampling distribution
- A statistic is ==biased== for a population parameter when the ==mean of its sampling distribution differs from that parameter==.

\[\text{Biased}\iff\mu_{\hat{\theta}}\ne\theta\]

- Here, \(\theta\) is the population parameter and \(\mu_{\hat{\theta}}\) is the mean of the estimator's sampling distribution.
- State which statistic estimates which parameter, then compare the sampling-distribution mean with the parameter's value.

### False positives and false negatives in a tree diagram
- Start with the given ==disease prevalence==, ==false positive rate==, and ==false negative rate==.
- Let \(D\) mean “has the disease,” \(+\) mean a positive result, and \(-\) mean a negative result.

| Given quantity | Probability |
| --- | --- |
| Disease prevalence | \(p=P(D)\) |
| False positive rate | \(\alpha=P(+\mid D^c)\) |
| False negative rate | \(\beta=P(-\mid D)\) |

- Split first into disease and no disease, with probabilities \(p\) and \(1-p\).
- From each group, split into positive and negative test results. Use the given rate and its complement for each pair of branches.
- Complete ==all four paths==. Label every branch probability and write its product at the end of the path.
