### Random variable and probability distribution {days=1}
- A **random variable** assigns a numerical value to each outcome of a random process.
- Its **probability distribution** lists the possible values and how likely they are.
- A discrete model is valid when every probability is between 0 and 1 and ==the probabilities sum to 1==.
- **PPT source** · Day 1, slides 6–7.

### Discrete and continuous random variables {days=1,3}
| Type | Possible values | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Discrete | A ==countable== set of values | Number of heads in three tosses |
| Continuous | Any value in an ==interval== | Time until a train arrives |

- Discrete probabilities are sums of probability masses. Continuous probabilities are areas under a density curve.
- **PPT source** · Day 1, slides 6–8, and Day 3, slide 2.

### Expected value {days=1}
- The expected value \(\mu_X=E(X)\) is the ==long-run average== value of \(X\) over many repetitions.
- It is a probability-weighted mean. It can fall between possible outcomes.
- **PPT source** · Day 1, slide 10.

### Variance and standard deviation {days=1}
- **Variance** \(\sigma_X^2\) is the probability-weighted average squared distance from the mean.
- **Standard deviation** \(\sigma_X\) is its square root. It measures a ==typical distance from the mean== in the original units of \(X\).
- **PPT source** · Day 1, slides 12–13.

### Parameters and statistics {days=1,6}
| Quantity | Population parameter | Sample statistic |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mean | \(\mu\) | \(\bar{x}\) |
| Standard deviation | \(\sigma\) | \(s\) |
| Proportion | \(p\) | \(\hat p\) |

- A parameter describes the ==population==. A statistic is calculated from a ==sample==.
- **PPT source** · Day 1, slide 18, and Day 6, slides 2–4.
