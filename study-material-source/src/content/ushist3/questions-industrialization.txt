### How did industrial growth change daily life from 1865 to 1900?
- More people moved from farms to cities. Electricity, telephones, and railroads changed how people lived, talked, and traveled.

### How did patents help people create new inventions?
- Patents gave inventors control of their ideas for a limited time. This encouraged people to invent and invest.

### How did Bell's telephone change communication?
- It let people talk over long distances and helped businesses communicate faster.

### Why was Edwin Drake's oil discovery in 1859 important?
- It helped start the oil industry. Oil provided kerosene for lamps and fuel for machines.

### How did the Bessemer process change cities and transportation?
- It made steel faster and cheaper to produce. Steel was used for railroads, bridges, and tall buildings.

### What was Taylor's scientific management?
- Taylor studied how workers did their jobs. He removed wasted steps and made work more efficient.

### How did Ford use Taylor's ideas in car factories?
- Ford used a moving assembly line. It made cars faster and cheaper, but the work was repetitive.

### What was one advantage of a corporation?
- A corporation could raise money by selling stock. Investors had limited personal responsibility for its debts.

### How did Rockefeller and Standard Oil limit competition?
- Rockefeller bought rival oil companies, got lower shipping rates, and cut prices. Standard Oil came to control most oil refining.

### How are horizontal and vertical integration different?
- Horizontal integration joins businesses at the ==same stage==. Vertical integration controls ==different stages of making and selling a product==.

### How did Carnegie use vertical integration?
- He controlled iron mines, coal mines, and transportation. This let his company manage more steps in steelmaking and lower costs.

### How did the government help industry despite laissez-faire?
- It gave land to railroads and used tariffs to protect U.S. businesses from foreign competition.

### How did corruption affect government oversight?
- Business leaders bribed politicians for land and helpful laws. This weakened government oversight.

### Why did the Sherman Antitrust Act fail to stop monopolies at first?
- The law was unclear, and courts read it narrowly. This made it hard to use against powerful companies.

### What did the E.C. Knight case decide?
- The Court said manufacturing was separate from interstate trade. This limited how the federal government could use the Sherman Act against manufacturers.

### Why did Mark Twain call this period the Gilded Age?
- The name means a shiny outside hid poverty, corruption, and inequality.

### How unequal were incomes?
- Very unequal. The guide compares Carnegie's 25 million dollars in 1890 with an average worker's 440 dollars a year.

### What did Carnegie argue in The Gospel of Wealth?
- He said wealthy people should use their money to help the public.

### Why did some people call business leaders robber barons?
- Critics said they mistreated workers, bribed politicians, and forced competitors out of business.

### Why did some people call business leaders captains of industry?
- Supporters said they created jobs, developed new ideas, and helped the economy grow.
