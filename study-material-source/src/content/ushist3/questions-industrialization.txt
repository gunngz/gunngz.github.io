### How did the shift from a rural society to an industrial power between 1865 and 1900 affect daily life and infrastructure?
- More Americans moved from rural communities to cities, while electricity, telephones, and railroads changed how people lived, communicated, and traveled.

### What role did patents play in driving technological innovation during the late 19th century?
- Patents protected inventors' ideas for a limited time, making it more worthwhile to invest in developing inventions and bringing them to market.

### How did Alexander Graham Bell's commercial telephone change American communication after its invention in 1876?
- The telephone let people speak over long distances, helping businesses communicate more efficiently and expanding access to rapid communication.

### Why was Edwin Drake's 1859 oil discovery in Titusville, Pennsylvania, historically significant?
- Drake's drilling helped launch the petroleum industry. Oil supplied kerosene for lamps and lubricants for machinery, and petroleum later supplied gasoline for automobiles.

### How did the Bessemer process change American infrastructure and city skylines?
- By making steel faster and cheaper to produce, it enabled wider use of steel in railroad rails, bridges, and tall buildings such as skyscrapers.

### What were the main principles of Frederick W. Taylor's scientific management?
- Taylor used time-and-motion studies to identify and remove unnecessary movements, standardize tasks, and increase worker speed and factory output.

### How did Henry Ford adapt Taylorism to change automobile manufacturing?
- Ford used a moving assembly line that brought work to stationary workers. It increased production and lowered car prices, while making many jobs repetitive.

### What advantages did the corporate business model offer over a traditional single-owner business?
- A corporation could raise large amounts of capital by selling stock. Its separate legal status also limited investors' personal financial liability.

### How did John D. Rockefeller and Standard Oil limit market competition?
- Rockefeller acquired competing refineries, used railroad shipping rebates, and cut prices to pressure rivals. Standard Oil controlled about 90% of U.S. oil refining by 1882.

### What is the key difference between horizontal and vertical integration?
- Horizontal integration combines competitors at the ==same production stage==. Vertical integration controls ==different stages of production and distribution==.

### How did Andrew Carnegie use vertical integration to dominate the steel industry?
- Carnegie acquired iron ore mines, coalfields, and transportation networks, giving his company control over many stages from raw materials to steel delivery and helping reduce costs.

### How did the federal government support industrial growth while following a laissez-faire doctrine?
- The government granted land for railroad construction, sold public land for resource development, and used protective tariffs to shield U.S. businesses from foreign competition.

### How did political corruption affect government oversight during rapid industrialization?
- Business leaders bribed politicians to obtain land and favorable laws. This weakened oversight and encouraged officials to serve private business interests.

### Why was the Sherman Antitrust Act initially ineffective at curbing monopolies?
- Its wording was vague, making it difficult to enforce against powerful corporations; courts also interpreted its reach narrowly.

### What was the legal significance of the Supreme Court's decision in United States v. E.C. Knight Co. (1895)?
- The Court treated manufacturing as separate from interstate commerce, limiting the federal government's ability to use the Sherman Act against manufacturing monopolies.

### Why did Mark Twain call the late 19th century the Gilded Age?
- The name suggested that a shiny surface of economic growth hid poverty, political corruption, and inequality.

### How extreme was income inequality in the late 19th century?
- It was very large: the guide's example compares Andrew Carnegie's $25 million in 1890 with an average worker's annual pay of $440.

### What main argument did Andrew Carnegie make in his 1889 essay, The Gospel of Wealth?
- Carnegie argued that wealthy people had a moral responsibility to use their fortunes to benefit the public rather than simply pass on or keep their wealth.

### What arguments supported calling business leaders of this era robber barons?
- Critics pointed to worker exploitation, unsafe workplaces, very low wages, political bribery, and aggressive practices that drove competitors out of business.

### What arguments supported calling business leaders of this era captains of industry?
- Supporters credited them with taking financial risks, introducing innovations, creating jobs, expanding production, and helping build a leading industrial economy.
