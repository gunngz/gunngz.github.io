### Capital
- Money and resources invested in a business to build factories, purchase machinery, and expand production.

### Patents
- Government protections that give an inventor the exclusive right to use or sell an invention for a limited time.

### Bessemer process
- A steelmaking method that blows air through molten iron to remove impurities, making steel faster and less expensive to produce.

### Scientific management (Taylorism)
- A system that studies and standardizes workers' movements to reduce wasted effort and increase factory output.

### Corporation
- A business legally separate from its owners that can raise money by selling stock; investors generally risk only the money they put in.

### Monopoly
- A company that controls most or nearly all of a market, leaving little competition.

### Horizontal integration
- Business expansion by buying or merging with competitors at the ==same stage of production==.

### Vertical integration
- Business expansion by controlling ==multiple stages of production and distribution==, from raw materials to delivery.

### Laissez-faire
- The belief that markets should operate with ==little government interference==.

### Sherman Antitrust Act (1890)
- A federal law intended to prohibit trusts, monopolies, and agreements that restrain trade.

### The Gilded Age
- Mark Twain's name for the late 19th century, when visible prosperity covered serious problems such as ==inequality and corruption==.

### Gospel of Wealth
- Andrew Carnegie's belief that wealthy people had a responsibility to use their fortunes for the public good.

### Robber barons
- A critical label for business leaders who gained wealth through practices such as worker exploitation, unsafe conditions, political bribery, and unfair competition.

### Captains of industry
- A positive label for business leaders credited with innovation, job creation, investment, and industrial growth.
