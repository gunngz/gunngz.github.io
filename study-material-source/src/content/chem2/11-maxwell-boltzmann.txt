### Maxwell–Boltzmann: three-step template
**Condition → Average kinetic energy and speed → Curve shape**

Use this template when the horizontal axis is ==speed==. Peak-height comparisons assume ==the same number of particles or normalized distributions==.

### Step 1 — Use temperature to compare average kinetic energy
**Same gas, different temperatures:**

> At a **[higher / lower] temperature**, the particles have **[greater / lower] average kinetic energy**.

**Different gases, the same temperature:**

> Both gases have the ==same average kinetic energy== because they are at the ==same temperature==.

### Step 2 — Explain speed and speed variability
**Heating the same gas — classroom wording:**

> Their average speed increases. More frequent collisions allow particles to exchange more energy, resulting in ==greater variability in particle speeds==.

**Different gases at the same temperature — use mass:**

> The gas with the ==smaller molar mass== has a ==greater average speed==, since its lighter particles must move faster to have the same average kinetic energy.

### Step 3 — Connect to the curve shape
> Therefore, the speed distribution shifts toward **[higher / lower] speeds** and becomes **[broader / narrower]**, with a **[lower / higher] peak**.

| Comparison | Direction | Width | Peak |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Same gas, higher temperature | Higher speeds | Broader | Lower |
| Same gas, lower temperature | Lower speeds | Narrower | Higher |
| Same temperature, smaller molar mass | Higher speeds | Broader | Lower |
| Same temperature, larger molar mass | Lower speeds | Narrower | Higher |

### If asked about area
**Same number of particles:**

> The total area remains unchanged because the ==number of particles is unchanged==.

**Normalized distributions:**

> The total areas are equal because ==both distributions are normalized==.

### Complete example — heating the same gas
> At a higher temperature, the particles have ==greater average kinetic energy== and a ==greater average speed==.
> More frequent collisions allow particles to exchange more energy, resulting in ==greater variability in particle speeds==.
> Therefore, the speed distribution shifts toward ==higher speeds== and becomes ==broader==, with a ==lower peak==.
> The total area remains unchanged because the ==number of particles is unchanged==.
