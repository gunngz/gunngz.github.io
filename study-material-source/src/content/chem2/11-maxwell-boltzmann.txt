### Speed-curve comparisons
| Comparison | Direction | Width | Peak |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Same gas, higher temperature | Higher speeds | Broader | Lower |
| Same gas, lower temperature | Lower speeds | Narrower | Higher |
| Same temperature, smaller molar mass | Higher speeds | Broader | Lower |
| Same temperature, larger molar mass | Lower speeds | Narrower | Higher |

![Four speed-distribution comparisons. Each panel compares a reference curve with the result of heating, cooling, decreasing molar mass, or increasing molar mass.](./diagrams/chem2-mb-shape-comparisons.svg)

### Energy-curve comparisons
Here, \(E_k\) is ==translational kinetic energy==.

| Comparison | Energy-distribution shape |
| --- | --- |
| Higher temperature | Shifts toward ==higher energies==; ==broader==, with a ==lower peak==. |
| Lower temperature | Shifts toward ==lower energies==; ==narrower==, with a ==higher peak==. |
| Same temperature, different molar masses | The normalized curves ==overlap==: same distribution, width, and peak. |

Key idea: ==Different speed distributions can have the same kinetic-energy distribution==. Read the horizontal axis before comparing curves.

### If asked about area
**Same number of particles:**

> The total area remains unchanged because the ==number of particles is unchanged==.

**Normalized distributions:**

> The total areas are equal because ==both distributions are normalized==.
