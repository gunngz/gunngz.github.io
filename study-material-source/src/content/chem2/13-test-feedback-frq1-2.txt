### FRQ 1(a): net ionic equation · 1 point
**Given**
Aqueous \(\mathrm{BaCl_2}\) and \(\mathrm{Na_2SO_4}\) are mixed. An initial particle model contains 4 \(\mathrm{Ba^{2+}}\) ions, 8 \(\mathrm{Cl^-}\) ions, 6 \(\mathrm{Na^+}\) ions, and 3 \(\mathrm{SO_4^{2-}}\) ions. Every symbol represents the same number of particles. Assume the precipitation reaction goes to completion and ignore the small amount of dissolved precipitate.

**Question**
Write the balanced net ionic equation, including states.

<!-- answer -->
**Scoring points**

1. Give the correct net ionic equation, including ==physical states==.

\[\mathrm{Ba^{2+}(aq)+SO_4^{2-}(aq)\rightarrow BaSO_4(s)}\]

### FRQ 1(e): wet precipitate · 2 points {priority}
**Given**
Aqueous \(\mathrm{BaCl_2}\) and \(\mathrm{Na_2SO_4}\) are mixed. An initial particle model contains 4 \(\mathrm{Ba^{2+}}\) ions, 8 \(\mathrm{Cl^-}\) ions, 6 \(\mathrm{Na^+}\) ions, and 3 \(\mathrm{SO_4^{2-}}\) ions. Every symbol represents the same number of particles. Assume the precipitation reaction goes to completion and ignore the small amount of dissolved precipitate.

**Question**
In a separate gravimetric determination of sulfate, all sulfate is precipitated as \(\mathrm{BaSO_4}\), but the precipitate is weighed while still wet. Is the calculated amount of sulfate too high, too low, or unchanged? Justify.

<!-- answer -->
**Scoring points**

1. State that the calculated sulfate amount is ==too high== because retained water increases the measured precipitate mass.
2. Trace the error: the overestimated mass gives too many moles of \(\mathrm{BaSO_4}\); the \(1:1\) mole ratio then gives ==too many moles of sulfate==.

### FRQ 2(a): redox in acidic solution · 2 points
**Given**
Two reactions involve permanganate ions. Show enough work to make your balancing and chemical reasoning clear. You may add \(\mathrm{H_2O}\), \(\mathrm{H^+}\), or \(\mathrm{OH^-}\) as appropriate to the stated conditions.

**Question**
Balance this equation in acidic solution:

\[\mathrm{MnO_4^-(aq)+Fe^{2+}(aq)\rightarrow Mn^{2+}(aq)+Fe^{3+}(aq)}\]

<!-- answer -->
**Scoring points**

1. Use the correct coefficients for \(\mathrm{MnO_4^-}\), \(\mathrm{Fe^{2+}}\), \(\mathrm{Mn^{2+}}\), and \(\mathrm{Fe^{3+}}\): **1 : 5 : 1 : 5**.
2. Add **8** \(\mathrm{H^+}\) on the left and **4** \(\mathrm{H_2O}\) on the right so that ==atoms and charge are balanced==.

\[\mathrm{MnO_4^-+8H^++5Fe^{2+}\rightarrow Mn^{2+}+5Fe^{3+}+4H_2O}\]

### FRQ 2(c): redox in basic solution · 2 points
**Given**
Two reactions involve permanganate ions. Show enough work to make your balancing and chemical reasoning clear. You may add \(\mathrm{H_2O}\), \(\mathrm{H^+}\), or \(\mathrm{OH^-}\) as appropriate to the stated conditions.

**Question**
Balance this equation in basic solution:

\[\mathrm{MnO_4^-(aq)+SO_3^{2-}(aq)\rightarrow MnO_2(s)+SO_4^{2-}(aq)}\]

<!-- answer -->
**Scoring points**

1. Use the correct coefficients for \(\mathrm{MnO_4^-}\), \(\mathrm{SO_3^{2-}}\), \(\mathrm{MnO_2}\), and \(\mathrm{SO_4^{2-}}\): **2 : 3 : 2 : 3**.
2. Add **1** \(\mathrm{H_2O}\) on the left and **2** \(\mathrm{OH^-}\) on the right. Balance atoms and charge, with ==no remaining hydrogen ions== in the final equation.

\[\mathrm{2MnO_4^-+3SO_3^{2-}+H_2O\rightarrow 2MnO_2+3SO_4^{2-}+2OH^-}\]

### FRQ 2(d): converting to basic conditions · 2 points {priority}
**Given**
Two reactions involve permanganate ions. Show enough work to make your balancing and chemical reasoning clear. You may add \(\mathrm{H_2O}\), \(\mathrm{H^+}\), or \(\mathrm{OH^-}\) as appropriate to the stated conditions.

**Question**
When converting an acidic half-reaction to basic conditions, why is the same number of \(\mathrm{OH^-}\) ions added to both sides? Explain what happens to the \(\mathrm{H^+}\) ions.

<!-- answer -->
**Scoring points**

1. Add the ==same number of hydroxide ions to both sides== to preserve atom and charge balance.
2. Combine \(\mathrm{H^+}\) and \(\mathrm{OH^-}\) to form water, then ==cancel water present on both sides==.
