### Lab-quiz response patterns
| Prompt type | Reasoning sequence |
| --- | --- |
| Chemical reaction | acid structure → ionizable \(\mathrm{H^+}\) → \(\mathrm{OH^-}\) → water → mole ratio |
| Calculation | given measurements → formula → balanced-equation mole ratio → result with units |
| Experimental error | error → affected measurement → calculated moles → final direction |
| Identify the acid | claim → experimental and accepted molar mass → structure and ionizable protons → observed properties |
| Evaluate reliability | precision and standard deviation → accuracy and percent error → possible error → specific improvement |

**Chemical-reaction template**

> Based on the ==acid structure==, each molecule donates ______ ionizable \(\mathrm{H^+}\) ions. These react with ==\(\mathrm{OH^-}\)== from \(\mathrm{NaOH}\) to form ==water==. The balanced equation gives a ==mole ratio of ______==, so ______.

**Calculation template**

> We calculated ______ using the ==given measurements== and ==formula==. Then we converted the moles of ______ to the moles of ______ using the balanced equation’s ==mole ratio==. Finally, we calculated ______ by dividing ______ by ______ and reported the ==result with units==.

**Error template**

> Because of the ==error==, the measured ==quantity== would be ==too high or too low==. This would cause the ==calculated moles== of ______ to be overestimated or underestimated. Therefore, the calculated molar mass would be too high or too low, showing the ==final direction== of the result.

**Identification template**

> Our ==claim== is that the unknown acid is ______ because its ==experimental molar mass== of ______ is close to the ==accepted molar mass== of ______. Its ==structure and ionizable protons== explain why it is ______. Its ==observed physical properties== also match our identification.

**Reliability template**

> Our results showed ______ ==precision==, indicated by a ==standard deviation== of ______. The experimental mean differed from the accepted value by ______%, the ==percent error==, indicating ______ ==accuracy== under our proposed identification. Possible errors include ______. We could make a ==specific improvement== by ______.

### Final check
| Check | Essential idea |
| --- | --- |
| Acid | Donates \(\mathrm{H^+}\) |
| Base | Accepts \(\mathrm{H^+}\) |
| Neutralization | \(\mathrm{H^+ + OH^- \rightarrow H_2O}\) |
| Diprotic acid | Two ionizable \(\mathrm{H^+}\) per acid formula unit |
| \(\mathrm{KHP}:\mathrm{NaOH}\) mole ratio | 1:1 |
| Unknown acid:\(\mathrm{NaOH}\) mole ratio | 1:2 |
| Standardize \(\mathrm{NaOH}\) | Determine its actual molarity |
| Endpoint vs. equivalence point | Indicator change vs. stoichiometric reaction point |
| Faint-pink endpoint | Avoid excess \(\mathrm{NaOH}\) |
| Oxalic acid dihydrate evidence | Molar mass, structure, and physical properties |
| Overshoot endpoint | Calculated molar mass too low |
| Lose acid during transfer | Calculated molar mass too high |
