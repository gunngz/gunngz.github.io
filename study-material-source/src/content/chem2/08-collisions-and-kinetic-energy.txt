### Collision template: explain why
Remember only two general reasoning templates. Fill in the specific changes from the question.

**Initial Change → Collisions → Final Result**

> As **[variable]** increases/decreases, particles collide **[how]**, resulting in **[final result]**.

**Choose the collision wording that fits the question:**

- ==More frequent collisions==
- ==More successful collisions==
- ==More collisions with container walls==

**Special case: a wider distribution (Q5)**

\(T\uparrow\) → More frequent collisions
→ More energy exchange → Greater speed variability

> More frequent collisions allow particles to exchange more energy, resulting in ==greater variability in particle speeds==.

Key idea: This explanation follows the teacher's emphasis on collisions. Strictly speaking, ==higher temperature is the underlying cause== of the wider distribution.

### Kinetic energy formula template
\[\boxed{KE=\frac{1}{2}mv^2}\]

\[\boxed{T\uparrow\Rightarrow\overline{KE}\uparrow}\]

**Remember:**

\[\boxed{\text{Same }T\Rightarrow\text{Same average }KE}\]

> At the same temperature, particles have the ==same average kinetic energy==. According to \(KE=\frac{1}{2}mv^2\), ==heavier particles move more slowly on average==.

**Choose between the two templates:**

| Question | Reasoning |
| --- | --- |
| Why / Explain, involving collisions | Initial change → Collisions → Result |
| Relationship between temperature, mass, speed, and KE | Temperature → Average KE → Formula → Result |

The question provides the specific scenario; you do not need to memorize a separate template for each one.
