### Three-sentence template
**Particle situation → Important factor → Pressure and Z**

| Step | Answer template |
| --- | --- |
| 1. Particle situation | At **[condition]**, gas particles **[describe their separation or motion]**. |
| 2. Important factor | Therefore, **[explain whether intermolecular attractions or particle volume are significant]**. |
| 3. Pressure and Z | At the ==same temperature, volume, and number of moles==, the pressure is **[lower than / higher than / approximately equal to]** the ideal prediction, so \(Z\) is **[less than / greater than / approximately equal to]** 1. |

### What to fill in for each situation
| Situation | Three-step reasoning |
| --- | --- |
| Low pressure | **1.** Particles are ==far apart==.<br>**2.** Attractions have little effect; particle volume is ==negligible compared with the container volume==.<br>**3.** \(P_{\mathrm{real}}\approx P_{\mathrm{ideal}}\)<br>\(Z\approx1\) |
| Attractions dominate | **1.** Particles are close enough for attractions to be significant.<br>**2.** Attractions ==reduce the pressure exerted on the walls==.<br>**3.** \(P_{\mathrm{real}}<P_{\mathrm{ideal}}\)<br>\(Z<1\) |
| Very high pressure: volume and repulsion effects dominate | **1.** Particles are ==very close together==.<br>**2.** ==Finite particle volume and short-range repulsions== are significant.<br>**3.** \(P_{\mathrm{real}}>P_{\mathrm{ideal}}\)<br>\(Z>1\) |

### Low-pressure example
> At low pressure, gas particles are ==far apart==.
> Intermolecular attractions have little effect, and the particles' own volume is ==negligible compared with the container volume==.
> At the ==same temperature, volume, and number of moles==, the pressure is approximately equal to the ideal prediction, so \(Z\) approaches 1.

**Volume wording:**

> The particles' own volume is negligible compared with the container volume.

Key idea: The total volume occupied by the particles themselves is very small compared with the entire container and can be ignored. The ==particles themselves have not become smaller==.

### Higher temperature: the same reasoning order
**Greater average kinetic energy → Attractions are relatively less significant → Smaller deviation caused by attractions**

> At higher temperatures, particles have ==greater average kinetic energy==.
> Intermolecular attractions are ==less significant relative to their motion==.
> Therefore, the ==deviation from ideal behavior caused by attractions becomes smaller==.
