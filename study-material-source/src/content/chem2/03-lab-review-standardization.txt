### Standardizing \(\mathrm{NaOH}\) with \(\mathrm{KHP}\)
The exact molarity of the \(\mathrm{NaOH}\) solution is not known at first. \(\mathrm{KHP}\) is used as a primary standard because it has a known molar mass, is stable, and can be accurately weighed. Its molar mass is \(204.22\ \mathrm{g/mol}\). \(\mathrm{KHP}\) is monoprotic, so it reacts with \(\mathrm{NaOH}\) in a 1:1 mole ratio.

| Step | Action |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Rinse the buret with deionized water, then rinse it with \(\mathrm{NaOH}\) to prevent dilution. |
| 2 | Fill the buret with \(\mathrm{NaOH}\). |
| 3 | Accurately weigh \(\mathrm{KHP}\) and dissolve it in water. |
| 4 | Add phenolphthalein and titrate while swirling. |
| 5 | Stop at a faint-pink endpoint and record the buret readings. |
| 6 | Repeat trials. |

**Answer template**

> We used \(\mathrm{KHP}\) as a ==stable primary standard== with a ==known molar mass==. We accurately measured its mass and dissolved it in distilled water. After adding phenolphthalein, we titrated the solution with \(\mathrm{NaOH}\) until a faint-pink endpoint appeared. Because \(\mathrm{KHP}\) is ==monoprotic== and reacts in a ==1:1 mole ratio== with \(\mathrm{NaOH}\), its mass and the volume delivered allow us to calculate the ==\(\mathrm{NaOH}\) molarity==.


### \(\mathrm{NaOH}\) standardization calculation
Example values only; these are not the class’s reported \(\mathrm{NaOH}\) concentration:

\[
m_{\mathrm{KHP}}=0.347\ \mathrm{g}
\qquad
V_{\mathrm{NaOH}}=20.80\ \mathrm{mL}
\]

\[
M_{\mathrm{NaOH}}
=
\frac{0.347\ \mathrm{g\ KHP}}{204.22\ \mathrm{g/mol\ KHP}}
\times
\frac{1\ \mathrm{mol\ NaOH}}{1\ \mathrm{mol\ KHP}}
\times
\frac{1\ \mathrm{L}}{0.02080\ \mathrm{L\ NaOH}}
=0.0817\ \mathrm{M}
\]

Use the measured \(\mathrm{KHP}\) mass, its molar mass, the 1:1 mole ratio, and the delivered \(\mathrm{NaOH}\) volume in liters.

**Answer template**

> First, ==convert the measured \(\mathrm{KHP}\) mass to moles==. Then apply the ==1:1 mole ratio==, convert the delivered \(\mathrm{NaOH}\) volume to ==liters==, and calculate molarity as ==moles per liter==.
