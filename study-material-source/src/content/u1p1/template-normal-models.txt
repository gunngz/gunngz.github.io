### How do you find a probability for a Normal random variable?
- “Let \(X\) be the **[variable]**. We want \(P(X\ [<,>,\text{ or between}]\ [value(s)])\).”
- “Using ==`normalcdf(lower, upper, mean, standard deviation)`==, the probability is ==**[area]**==.”
- “About **[100 × area]%** of **[population or outcomes]** are **[below/above/between] [value(s)]**.”
- ✓ Use \(-\infty\) or \(+\infty\) as a calculator bound when the question asks for one tail.

![Example of a probability sketch with lower and upper bounds and the shaded area.](./diagrams/u1p1-normal-probability.svg)

### How do you find a value from a percentile in a Normal model?
- “The **[percentile]th** percentile corresponds to cumulative area ==**[area]**==.”
- “Using ==`inverseNorm(area, mean, standard deviation)`==, the value is **[value and units]**.”
- Check that the requested area is ==to the left of the value==; convert an upper-tail percentage to a left-tail area when needed.

![Example of a percentile sketch with cumulative area to the left of the cutoff.](./diagrams/u1p1-normal-percentile.svg)

### How do you assess whether a Normal model is reasonable using an NPP?
- “The points in the Normal probability plot are ==**[roughly linear / clearly curved or far from linear]**==.”
- “Therefore, a Normal model ==**[is reasonable / is not reasonable]**== for **[variable]**.”
- ✓ Support the conclusion with the pattern's linearity; do not rely only on the plot's title.

![A roughly linear Normal probability plot supports a Normal model; a curved one does not.](./diagrams/u1p1-normal-npp.svg)

### What should a sketch of a Normal distribution include?
- Draw a ==symmetric bell-shaped curve== centered at **[mean]**.
- Label **[mean]** and the values ==[mean − 3 SD], [mean − 2 SD], [mean − SD], [mean + SD], [mean + 2 SD], and [mean + 3 SD]==.
- Shade the region corresponding to ==**[probability or percentile]**== and label it with \(P([event])=[area]\).
- ✓ The total area under a Normal curve is 1; shaded area represents probability.

![A labeled Normal curve with standard-deviation marks and the requested region shaded.](./diagrams/u1p1-normal-sketch.svg)

### How do you compare two Normal curves with different standard deviations?
- “Both curves have ==the same mean== **[mean]**. The curve with standard deviation **[smaller/larger]** is **[==taller and narrower== / shorter and wider]**.”
- ✓ When the means match, the smaller standard deviation gives a taller, narrower curve.

![Two Normal curves with the same mean and different standard deviations.](./diagrams/u1p1-normal-spread.svg)
