### Comparative experiment {days=3}

- **Definition:** An experiment that compares responses from units receiving two or more treatments. A ==control group== may provide a baseline.
- **Key idea:** Keep groups alike except for treatment. If only one group receives treatment, time changes, expectations, or other shared factors may be mistaken for a treatment effect.

### Treatment {days=3}

- **Definition:** A specific condition imposed on experimental units. With multiple factors, one treatment is a particular combination of factor levels.
- **Key idea:** List the combinations of factor levels that experimental units receive.

### ==Control group== {days=3}

- **Definition:** A comparison group that provides a baseline for judging the effects of other treatments. It may receive no treatment, standard care, an active treatment, or a placebo.
- **Key idea:** Treat groups alike in all other ways so their responses can be compared.

### Placebo {days=3}

- **Definition:** An inactive treatment designed to resemble the active treatment.
- **Key idea:** A placebo can provide a baseline and help control changes caused by participants’ expectations.

### Placebo effect {days=3}

- **Definition:** Improvement or another response caused by a subject’s expectation of treatment rather than by the active treatment itself.
- !**Clue:** Participants may respond because they expect a treatment to work. A similar-looking placebo and blinding can help control this effect.

### Blinding / single-blind / double-blind {days=3}

- **Definition:** Blinding means keeping knowledge of treatment assignment from people whose expectations could affect responses or measurements.
- **Double-blind:** Neither participants nor people interacting with or measuring them know the treatment assignments.
- **Single-blind:** One of those groups is unaware of the treatment assignments.
- !**Clue:** Masking participants’ assignments can balance expectations. Masking evaluators can reduce subjective measurement or interaction effects; side effects may reveal assignments.

### ==Random assignment== {days=3}

- **Definition:** Using chance to assign experimental units to treatment groups.
- ✓**Key idea:** ==Random assignment== tends to balance other variables across groups and reduce confounding. It does not guarantee identical groups or support generalization by itself.

### Completely randomized design (CRD) {days=3}

- **Definition:** An experiment in which all experimental units are assigned to the treatment groups completely at random, without first sorting them into blocks.
- **Key idea:** Assign all experimental units to groups at once. If units are first grouped by a characteristic and then randomized within groups, use a randomized block design.

### Replication {days=3}

- **Definition:** Assigning multiple experimental units to each treatment.
- **Key idea:** Use multiple experimental units per treatment to assess the treatment effect; repeating measurements on one unit is not replication.

### Direct control {days=3}

- **Definition:** Holding an extraneous variable constant across experimental units whenever possible.
- **Key idea:** Keep conditions such as time, location, procedure, temperature, or duration the same to reduce variability and confounding.

### Blocking variable / block {days=3}

- **Definition:** A blocking variable is a characteristic used to group experimental units that are similar and is believed to be ==associated with== the response.
- Each such group is a block.
- **Key idea:** Units within a block are similar and blocks differ. Compare treatments ==within each block==; this differs from sampling strata, where units are sampled from each group.

### Randomized block design {days=3}

- **Definition:** Group similar experimental units into blocks, then randomly assign units within every block to each treatment.
- **Key idea:** Block on a known characteristic related to the response, then randomize treatments ==within each block==.

### Matched pairs design {days=3}

- **Definition:** A randomized block design with blocks of two units, or one unit that receives both treatments in a random order.
- **Key idea:** Use both treatments on the same unit or pair similar units. Randomize treatment order to reduce time, practice, or carryover effects.

### Factorial experiment {days=3}

- **Definition:** An experiment that studies two or more factors by using combinations of their levels as treatments.
- With two factors and two levels each, a full factorial design has \(2 \times 2 = 4\) treatment combinations.

### Order effect / carryover effect {days=3}

- **Definition:** A response to a treatment sequence can be affected by which treatment came first or by an earlier treatment continuing to affect the later response.
- !**Clue:** When a unit receives multiple treatments, randomize the order to keep treatment from being ==confounded with== time, practice, or carryover.
