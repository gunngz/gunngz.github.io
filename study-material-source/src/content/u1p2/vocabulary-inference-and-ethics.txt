### ==Statistically significant== {days=4}

- **Definition:** An observed treatment difference is ==statistically significant== when it is so unusual that chance variation from ==random assignment== alone is not a plausible explanation.
- !**Clue:** Statistical significance does not mean a result is important or large; assess how unusual the result would be under ==random assignment== alone.

### Randomization distribution {days=4}

- **Definition:** A distribution of simulated statistics generated by repeatedly reassigning treatments ==under the assumption that== treatment has no effect.
- !**Clue:** Use the distribution to assess how unusual the observed statistic would be under random-assignment variation alone.

### Scope of inference {days=4}

- **Definition:** The conclusions a study supports, determined by how its observational or experimental units were selected and how treatments were assigned.
- ✓==random assignment== in a well-designed experiment plus statistical significance supports cause-and-effect inference.
- One kind of randomization does not substitute for the other.
- !**Clue:** ==Random selection== supports generalization to the sampled-from population; ==random assignment== supports cause-and-effect inference.

### Institutional Review Board (IRB) {days=4}

- **Definition:** A committee that reviews research involving human participants before the study begins.
- **Key idea:** Human-subject research should be reviewed before the study begins.

### Informed consent {days=4}

- **Definition:** Participants are told the relevant nature and risks of a study and agree to take part before participating.
- !**Clue:** Participants must understand the relevant purpose, procedures, and risks and agree before taking part.

### Confidentiality {days=4}

- **Definition:** Keeping participants’ identifiable information and individual responses private.
- !**Clue:** Protect identifiable information and individual responses; using personal data without informed consent can also raise ethical concerns.

### Anonymity vs. confidentiality {days=4}

- **Anonymity:** A respondent’s identity is not known or recorded with their answers.
- **Confidentiality:** Researchers may know or retain identity-linked information, but protect it from disclosure.
- **Key idea:** Confidential does not mean anonymous. Share group summaries, not identifiable individual data.

### Fair treatment of research participants {days=4}

- Human studies should distribute research burdens and benefits fairly.
- Withholding a valuable service from comparable controls can raise a fairness concern.
- !**Clue:** Identify who receives or is denied a benefit, whether there is a fair reason, and how the decision affects participants. ==Random assignment== alone does not resolve fairness.
