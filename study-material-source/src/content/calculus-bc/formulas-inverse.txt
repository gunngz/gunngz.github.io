### Derivative of an inverse function
Let \(g=f^{-1}\). If a local inverse exists and \(f'(g(a))\ne0\):

\[g'(a)=\frac{1}{f'(g(a))}\]

1. Find ==\(g(a)\)== by solving \(f(g(a))=a\).
2. Evaluate ==\(f'\) at \(g(a)\)==.
3. Take the ==reciprocal==.

- ! **Key idea:** The denominator is \(f'(g(a))\), not \(f'(a)\).
- An inverse function \(f^{-1}(x)\) is **not** the reciprocal \(1/f(x)\).

### Inverse derivative · 2003 AB #27
Given:

\[f(x)=x^3+x\]

\[g=f^{-1},\qquad g(2)=1\]

Find \(g'(2)\).

<!-- answer -->
1. **Differentiate the original function.**

   \[f'(x)=3x^2+1\]

2. **Evaluate at \(g(2)=1\).**

   \[f'(g(2))=f'(1)=3(1)^2+1=4\]

3. **Take the reciprocal.**

   \[\boxed{g'(2)=\frac14}\]
