### Check the graph label first
| Graph shown | What a crossing of the horizontal axis tells you |
| --- | --- |
| \(f\) | The function value is \(0\). This alone does not show a local extremum or inflection point. |
| \(f'\) | A ==sign change== identifies a local maximum or minimum of \(f\), provided \(f\) is continuous there. |
| \(f''\) | A ==sign change== identifies a change in concavity of \(f\). Check continuity of \(f\) for an inflection point. |

### Derivative signs and function behavior
| Condition on an interval | Behavior of \(f\) |
| --- | --- |
| \(f'(x)>0\) | ==Increasing== |
| \(f'(x)<0\) | ==Decreasing== |
| \(f''(x)>0\) | ==Concave up== |
| \(f''(x)<0\) | ==Concave down== |

### Sign changes identify turning points
| Test near \(x=c\) | Conclusion about \(f\) |
| --- | --- |
| \(f'\) changes from \(+\) to \(-\) | ==Local maximum== at \(c\), if \(f\) is continuous at \(c\). |
| \(f'\) changes from \(-\) to \(+\) | ==Local minimum== at \(c\), if \(f\) is continuous at \(c\). |
| \(f''\) changes sign | ==Inflection point== at \(c\), if \(f\) is continuous at \(c\). |

### Zero alone is not enough
- \(f'(c)=0\) alone does **not** prove a local maximum or minimum. Check the ==sign of \(f'\) on both sides==.
- \(f''(c)=0\) alone does **not** prove an inflection point. Check for a ==change in concavity==.
- In the review's **2003 AB #21** description, \(f''\) crosses the horizontal axis at \(a\) and \(0\), but only touches it at \(b\). The sign changes at \(a\) and \(0\); it does not change at \(b\).
