### FTC Part 1 with the chain rule
For continuous \(f\), a constant lower limit \(a\), and a differentiable upper limit \(g(x)\):

\[\frac{d}{dx}\int_a^{g(x)}f(t)\,dt=f(g(x))\,g'(x)\]

1. ==Substitute the upper limit== into the integrand.
2. ==Multiply by the derivative of the upper limit==.

When the upper limit is simply \(x\):

\[\frac{d}{dx}\int_a^x f(t)\,dt=f(x)\]

- ! **Key idea:** You do not need to evaluate the integral. Do not differentiate the integrand again.

### Both limits depend on x
For continuous \(f\) and differentiable limits \(u(x)\) and \(v(x)\):

\[\begin{aligned}\frac{d}{dx}\int_{u(x)}^{v(x)}f(t)\,dt
&=f(v(x))\,v'(x)\\
&\quad-f(u(x))\,u'(x)\end{aligned}\]

- ==Upper-limit contribution minus lower-limit contribution==.
- Apply the chain rule to **each** limit.

### Variable upper limit · 2003 AB #23
Find the derivative:

\[\frac{d}{dx}\left(\int_0^{x^2}\sin(t^3)\,dt\right)\]

<!-- answer -->
1. **Substitute the upper limit.**

   \[\sin\bigl((x^2)^3\bigr)=\sin(x^6)\]

2. **Multiply by its derivative.**

   \[\frac{d}{dx}(x^2)=2x\]

3. **Combine the factors.**

   \[\boxed{2x\sin(x^6)}\]

- **Answer: E** in the supplied review.
- ! **Key idea:** Keep the factor \(2x\), and simplify \((x^2)^3\) to \(x^6\).
